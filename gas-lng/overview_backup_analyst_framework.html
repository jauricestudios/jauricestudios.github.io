UK Gas Balance, Curve and Physical Optionality Study#

Purpose#

This project studies UK and European gas markets through physical balance, forward curves, logistics, storage and market risk.

The aim is not to build a trading system. The aim is to show how public data can be structured to understand where physical tightness, regional spreads, storage value and logistics constraints may create commercial risk or opportunity.

In physical commodity markets, the useful signal rarely comes from one dataset. It usually comes from combining flows, inventories, prices, curves, infrastructure, credit conditions, freight, policy and local market intelligence. This project is a public-data version of that thinking.

Market question#

Is the UK gas market tightening or loosening, and what do storage levels, LNG send-out, interconnector flows, NBP/TTF spreads and forward curve structure suggest about physical market risk?

Commercial lens#

This project is organised around six questions:

  1. Balance: Is supply greater than demand, or is the system relying on storage withdrawal, LNG send-out or imports?

  2. Curve: Is the forward curve pricing future scarcity, storage value or seasonal risk?

  3. Spread: Is the UK market cheap or expensive relative to continental Europe through the NBP/TTF spread?

  4. Logistics: Are LNG terminals, interconnectors, storage facilities or weather demand creating bottlenecks?

  5. Optionality: Does storage, flexible LNG supply or gas for power create option value under stress?

  6. Risk: What could break the view, such as cold weather, low wind, weak LNG arrivals, Norwegian flow disruption or policy shocks?

Why this matters#

In physical commodity markets, value is often created by moving supply from lower-value locations or periods into higher-value markets or periods. That requires more than a price view. It requires understanding where supply is available, where demand is short, whether infrastructure can move the commodity, whether storage has value, and whether buyers are paying premiums for reliability, timing, credit or delivery flexibility.

UK gas is a useful case study because it links LNG imports, European pipeline flows, storage, weather demand, power market stress and regional price spreads.

Proposed outputs#

This study will build the following outputs:

  1. UK gas balance table showing supply, demand and net tightness.

  2. NBP/TTF spread note explaining whether the UK is cheap or expensive relative to Europe.

  3. Forward curve interpretation focused on seasonal scarcity and storage value.

  4. Storage and LNG stress table showing how low storage or weak LNG send-out changes market risk.

  5. Infrastructure map showing LNG terminals, interconnectors and storage assets.

  6. Short analyst note summarising what changed, why it matters and what to watch next.

Analyst structure#

Each update should follow this structure:

  1. What changed?

  2. Which indicator moved?

  3. Why does it matter physically?

  4. How could it affect prices, spreads or storage value?

  5. What is uncertain?

  6. What should be watched next?

Limitations#

This is a student research portfolio built from public data. It does not include proprietary trader information such as live bids and offers, private counterparty behaviour, internal inventory data, credit lines, freight negotiations or physical access to terminals and storage.

The purpose is not to claim a trading edge. The purpose is to show a disciplined approach to physical commodity market research using public sources, transparent assumptions and clear interpretation.

Next step#

The next output is a simple UK gas balance table and source table. After that, the project will add an NBP/TTF spread chart and a short forward curve interpretation note.
